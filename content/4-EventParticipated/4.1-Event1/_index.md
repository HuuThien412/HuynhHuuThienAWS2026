# Event 1

**Event time:** Around 09:00 - 12:00

## 9:00 - 9:45 AM | Effective Learning Method and Learning Motivation

**Speaker: Mr. Hoang Long**

- Presented how to learn properly and avoid unfocused learning or following instructions without understanding the purpose.
- Explained the importance of setting clear learning goals before starting a new topic.
- Shared how to maintain learning motivation by breaking goals into smaller stages.
- Emphasized that learning should be combined with hands-on practice instead of only reading theory.
- Encouraged learners to record errors and solutions during practice.
- Suggested connecting learned knowledge with a practical project to make learning easier to remember and more motivating.

## 9:45 - 10:30 AM | Automated Prompt Engineering: Enhancing LLM Output Quality

**Speaker: Mr. Thinh Nguyen**

- Introduced **Prompt Engineering** as an important skill for communicating effectively with AI.
- Explained why generic prompts often lead to generic, unfocused, and less useful outputs.
- Presented common problems caused by poor prompts, including wasted tokens, higher costs, ambiguous instructions, and inconsistent results.
- Compared simple prompts with improved prompts that clearly define audience, length, goal, tone, and output format.
- Introduced the key components of a good prompt: **Role**, **Instruction**, **Context**, **Input Data**, **Output Format**, **Examples**, and **Constraints/Guidelines**.
- Shared prompting principles such as being clear and specific, using directive language, using delimiters, splitting long inputs into smaller sections, and allowing “I don’t know” responses when information is insufficient.
- Explained **Token Economics**, helping learners understand that long or poorly optimized prompts can increase token usage and AI cost.
- Introduced advanced techniques such as **Chain-of-Thought**, **Self-Consistency**, **Tree-of-Thoughts**, **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**, and **Role Prompting**.
- Introduced **Proptimizer**, a browser extension idea that optimizes prompts and allows users to chat with AI anywhere on the web.
- Presented the Proptimizer architecture using AWS services such as **Amazon S3**, **Amazon CloudFront**, **Amazon Cognito**, **Amazon API Gateway**, **AWS Lambda**, **Amazon Bedrock**, **Amazon DynamoDB**, and **Amazon CloudWatch**.

## 10:30 - 11:15 AM | Learning and Working in the Banking Environment

**Speaker: VIB group**

- Shared experiences about studying and working in the banking environment.
- Explained how technology knowledge can be applied to real work in banking.
- Discussed the importance of accuracy, security, process compliance, and responsibility.
- Emphasized self-learning, teamwork, and professional attitude.
- Helped attendees understand more about career opportunities in banking technology.

## 11:15 AM - 12:00 PM | Recruitment Experience and HR Perspective

**Speaker: Recruitment/HR representative**

- Shared how HR evaluates candidates during recruitment.
- Explained important factors such as learning attitude, communication skill, project experience, and teamwork.
- Emphasized the importance of CV preparation and presenting personal strengths clearly.
- Shared interview tips, especially answering questions clearly and honestly.
- Helped attendees understand how to prepare their profile, soft skills, and career direction after the internship.

## Key Takeaways

- I understood that AWS learning requires a clear method, specific goals, and regular practice.
- I realized that a worklog is not only a report requirement, but also a tool for tracking learning progress.
- I learned to view each AWS service through its purpose, workflow, and role in the overall architecture.
- I understood the role of Prompt Engineering in using AI for learning, documentation, debugging, and improving work efficiency.
- I learned that a good prompt should include context, clear instructions, expected output format, and specific constraints.
- I realized that prompt optimization can also help control cost when working with token-based AI models.
- I gained another perspective on building an AI application on AWS through the Proptimizer architecture.
- I learned more about the banking work environment and how security, process, and responsibility affect technology work.
- I understood more about how HR evaluates candidates and how to prepare a CV, interview answers, and soft skills.

## Connection to My Internship

The event content was directly related to my internship process. While learning AWS services and building the **Campus IT Support Ticket Portal** project, I needed to divide learning topics into smaller parts, record errors, test step by step, and connect knowledge with the actual system.

In addition, Mr. Thinh Nguyen's Prompt Engineering presentation helped me understand how to use AI more effectively during learning and documentation. Instead of asking generic questions, I can provide a clear role, context, input data, and expected format to receive more useful answers. The VIB group and HR sharing sessions also gave me a more practical view of working environments, professional expectations, and career preparation after the internship.

## Conclusion

Event 1 helped me improve my learning method, AI usage, and career preparation. Mr. Hoang Long's sharing helped me understand the importance of learning methods and motivation. Mr. Thinh Nguyen's presentation helped me better understand Prompt Engineering, tokens, advanced prompting techniques, and how an AI application can be built on AWS. The final two sessions helped me understand more about banking technology work and the factors HR usually considers during recruitment.
